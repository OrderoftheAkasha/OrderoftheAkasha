## Step 0: Get legal before selling to restaurants and stores (do first)

From the **Ohio Department of Agriculture Mushroom Fact Sheet (March 2026)**:
- **Restaurants and grocery stores:** any sale to an Ohio-licensed restaurant or retail food store requires your mushrooms to be **inspected under the Produce Safety Rule by ODA Food Safety**. That's what makes you an "approved source." **The inspection is free.**
- **Farmers markets / direct to people:** under **$25,000/year** in produce sales (3-year average), you can sell raw mushrooms to individuals **without** an inspection. Between $25K and $500K, you may qualify for an exemption with record-keeping.
- **Packaged mushrooms** (clamshells, bags with labels) must follow ODA labeling rules.
- **Processing** (slicing, drying, powders, tinctures, coffee) needs a separate ODA inspection.
- **Contact:** Matt Fout, ODA Food Safety · mfout@agri.ohio.gov · (614) 600-4272

Also get **product liability insurance** (restaurants and markets ask for a certificate), and register your business name if you haven't.

> Restaurants will ask, "Are you inspected?" Being able to say yes is itself a selling point. Put "ODA Produce Safety inspected" on your price sheet **only after** you pass.

## Step 2: Restaurants (the steady, recurring money)

### Who to target first
Pick **15 Cleveland restaurants** that already brag about local sourcing: farm-to-table, chef-driven menus, vegan and vegetarian spots (lion's mane replaces crab and meat), ramen and Asian kitchens (oyster), and upscale Italian and French (chestnut). Good areas: Ohio City, Tremont, Gordon Square, Downtown, Hingetown, Cleveland Heights, Lakewood.

### How to pitch (in person beats email)
1. Visit **Tuesday–Thursday, 2–4 PM** (between lunch and dinner). Ask for the chef or kitchen manager.
2. Bring a **free sample box** (about ½ lb of each variety) plus the **Wholesale Menu PDF**.
3. Say this: *"I grow gourmet mushrooms here in Cleveland: lion's mane, chestnut and oyster. Harvested the day before delivery. Here's a sample for your team to cook with. I do weekly, bi-weekly or monthly standing orders."*
4. Follow up **3 days later**: "What did the team make with them?" Then offer a **trial order**.
5. Convert to a **standing order**. The weekly tier is priced lowest on purpose, to lock in recurring revenue.

### Keep them forever
- Deliver on the **same day every week**, always on time.
- Send a text **the day before** with what's available and let them add on.
- Feature the restaurant online ("Find our lion's mane on the menu at ___"). Chefs love free promo, and their customers find you.
- Ask happy chefs for a **one-line quote** you can use on your price sheet and site.

**Email/DM version:**
> Subject: Cleveland-grown gourmet mushrooms for [Restaurant]
> Hi Chef [Name], I'm with Mega Mushrooms. We grow lion's mane, chestnut and oyster mushrooms right here in Cleveland and deliver within a day of harvest. I'd love to drop off a free sample box for your team this week. Standing orders are available weekly, bi-weekly or monthly. Price sheet attached. [Your name · phone]

## Step 3: Farmers markets (cash, brand building, chef discovery)

### Where to apply
- **North Union Farmers Market** runs multiple Cleveland markets and is producer-only (you must grow what you sell). Submit the **2026 New Vendor Interest Form**: northunionfarmersmarket.org/become-a-vendor
  - **Coit Road Farmers Market**: Saturdays **year-round**, 8 AM–1 PM. Great for winter income.
  - **Tremont Farmers Market**: Tuesdays 4–7 PM (May–Oct).
  - **Downtown Mall B market**: Tuesdays through Oct 6, 2026, 10:30 AM–1:30 PM.
- Also check the other markets in Cleveland Magazine's farmers market guide, plus suburban markets (Lakewood, Shaker, Chagrin Falls, Hudson).

### Win the table
- **Display:** baskets of whole clusters. Big lion's mane heads draw a crowd, so put one front and center.
- **Sell in ½ lb and 1 lb bags**, with prices on the Market Price Sign.
- **Recipe cards** with every purchase, printed with a QR code to your recipes page (builds followers).
- **Cook samples** if the market allows it: seared lion's mane "scallops" sell themselves.
- **Every market:** collect emails and phone numbers, hand chefs a Wholesale Menu, and post "We're at Coit Road today" stories.

## Step 6: Long-term growth (months 6–24)

1. **Subscription boxes:** a weekly or bi-weekly home delivery box (mix of all three plus a recipe card).
2. **Grow kits** for home growers. They sell well at markets and online.
3. **New varieties** once chefs trust you: pink oyster, king trumpet, shiitake, maitake.
4. **Value-added products** (dried mushrooms, seasoning blends). These need the ODA processing inspection first.
5. **Events:** a Mega Mushrooms pop-up dinner with a partner chef, or a mushroom-themed night at a brewery.
6. **Wholesale to distributors** once you have the volume and consistency.
