From DarkLight to the DarkLight Halo: How Akashic Monk's Albums and Novels Tell One Story

In 2020, Cleveland hip-hop artist Akashic Monk released DarkLight, a 13-track project with songs like "The TakeOver," "Black Budget," "New Me" and "Kiss of Bliss." A few years later, the name came back in a new form: the DarkLight Halo, the power wielded by Akir, the hero of the Monk's fantasy novel Order of the Akasha: The Book of Scrolls.

That's not a coincidence. It's a pattern. Across fifteen years of making music and 41 official releases since 2016, Akashic Monk has been building a single universe, one album and one chapter at a time.

  • DarkLight (2020) introduced the light-versus-dark duality that now defines Akir's power.
  • Soul Savior (2020) leaned into faith, survival and rebirth, with tracks like "Forged in the Fire," "Save Me" and "Eden."
  • Thoughts of a Wizard (2022) went fully mystic, with "Dreams 2 Reality," "Space Clouds" and "Super Powers."
  • Static Signal (2026) returned with 22 tracks, including "Spiritual Thesis" and "Return of the Blueprint."
  • Order of the Akasha: The Book of Scrolls and Origin's of Akir turned those themes into characters, relics and realms.

The name itself ties it together. Akasha is the ancient idea of a cosmic record of all knowledge. In the novels it's the fractured force holding every dimension together, and in the music it's the "Akashic" frequency the Monk keeps returning to.

Next up: the album American Blueprint (Winter 2026), and the continued build-out of Order of the Akasha into games and film.
